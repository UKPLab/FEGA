Sparse Autoencoders Encode Both Concepts and Functions: The Downstream Geometry of Feature Effects

Phu Gia Hoang Anwoy Chatterjee Tanmoy Chakraborty Iryna Gurevych Subhabrata Dutta

arXiv 2026 · Feature-Effect Geometry Analysis (FEGA)

Paper Code Explore FEGA

From a feature to its effects

A feature is not always a reusable direction.

FEGA removes one active SAE latent across many contexts, measures the resulting logit-effect vectors, and studies the geometry of that cloud in output space.

Abstract

Feature-Effect Geometry Analysis (FEGA) tests how a sparse autoencoder feature changes a language model’s output across many contexts. Rather than collapsing the feature to one steering vector, it represents its downstream logit effects as an empirical cloud and asks whether that cloud is one-dimensional, low-dimensional, clustered, or diffuse.

Across SAE variants, clean one-dimensional effects are rare. FEGA distinguishes value-like features, associated with more structured effects, from pointer-like features, whose context-dependent operations tend to yield diffuse geometry. This connects feature geometry, steering, and semantic multiplicity.
